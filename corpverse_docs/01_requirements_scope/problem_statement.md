# Problem Statement

## The Problem

Most students and early-career job seekers get almost no practical exposure to what a real hiring pipeline or a real workplace actually feels like before they're in it. Career prep today is fragmented: mock interview apps only cover interviews, resume tools only cover resumes, and project-based learning platforms don't simulate the actual back-and-forth of applying, getting rejected, improving, working under a manager, and growing through a career. Nobody offers the full loop in one place — including the entrepreneurial side, where you eventually might hire others yourself.

## Who This Is For

- **College students (final year especially)** who want low-stakes practice with resumes, applications, and interviews before facing the real job market.
- **Early professionals** exploring what career growth (promotions, raises, switching roles) actually looks like in a structured way.
- **Aspiring founders** who want to practice building and running a team before doing it with real money and real stakes.

## Why Now

- Final-year students (our own team included) consistently report feeling unprepared for the actual mechanics of job hunting — not the technical skill gap, but the process gap: how screening works, what interviewers actually evaluate, how promotions get decided, what running a team involves.
- LLMs have matured enough to realistically play the role of an interviewer, a hiring manager, or even a "founder" running a company — this wasn't practically possible a couple of years ago.
- EdTech is shifting toward simulation-based learning (practicing the real process) rather than static content (reading about the process) — CorpVerse fits squarely into that shift.

## What We're Solving

CorpVerse gives users a single platform to experience the entire professional lifecycle — job search, screening, interviews, employment, performance growth, and eventually founding and running their own company — in a gamified, low-stakes environment, with structured feedback at every rejection or setback so the experience is actually educational, not just pass/fail.
